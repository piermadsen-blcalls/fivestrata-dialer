02What is it

Three things at once.

The floor

A flexible outbound dialer

Campaigns, lists, cadences, caller-ID pools, dispositions — the full working vocabulary of a call-center floor, running with no humans on it. Same words the existing floors use, so results compare one-to-one.

The agent

An LLM-operated soundboard

It emulates how human soundboard call centers actually work — an operator picking the right pre-recorded line at the right moment. Here the operator is a language model, choosing each clip in real time and speaking in a natural, consistent voice.

The loop

A self-learning back end

Every turn of every call is logged: what the agent knew, what she chose, how the prospect responded. Nightly analysis turns her best improvised lines into tomorrow's clips, and measures every change against test batteries.

03How

Four proven platforms, one thin layer of ours.

The brain
Supabase

Queue, routing, live call state, and every control switch. The conversation engine itself runs serverless at the edge, co-located next to the carrier — no server of ours in the call path.

The voice
Telnyx

Carrier, phone numbers, call control, live transcription, recording — plus hosted language models and text-to-speech under the same account. One vendor, one bill, one API key.

The controls
Vercel

The configuration console and performance dashboards: programs, pacing knobs, script and clip management, per-call drill-down. Every write is audited; deployed and live today.

The memory
Snowflake

The long-term warehouse — every dial and every turn, retained five years. This is where optimization and machine learning live. The pipe is proven: 242,690 call events synced at full volume.

Anything else?  That's the whole stack. Transcription (Deepgram) and the LLMs ride the Telnyx account — no extra AI vendors, no per-seat licensing — and the FiveStrata integration surface (lead push, DNC, pre-auth, disposition write-back) is our own code, already built and live.
04What makes it different

Built like a floor, not like a chatbot.

i

Voice packs

The agent's voice, greeting, and delivery are swappable per call. A new voice — or an A/B test between two — is configuration, not code.

ii

Campaign & script ingestion

Production script workbooks go in; gated scripts and studio-quality clip sets come out. New vertical, new tenant — same pipeline.

iii

Turn-level call data

For every turn: what the AI knew → what it chose → how the prospect responded. Strictly richer than anything the human floors report today.

iv

Live TTS on demand

When no clip fits, she composes a constrained line in her own voice — capped, guarded, and logged. She is never stuck without an apt answer.

v

Hard requirements, built in

Recorded-line disclosure and consent are structural must-hit gates that testing can't remove. Opt-outs and DNC take effect immediately.

vi

Proper DID cycling

Numbers bought as scattered singles, screened before first dial, registered, health-monitored with automatic quarantine, retired on schedule.

vii

A synthetic test floor

The agent dials herself in character — skeptics, ramblers, price shoppers. Hundreds of test calls battle-test every change before a human ever hears it.

viii

Knows when to hang up

A live monitor estimates convert-probability every turn and politely ends dead-end calls — in testing, minutes saved with zero buyers lost.

08FAQ

Ask us the hard ones.

Click a question.
What technology is this built on?
Three commodity platforms and our own logic on top. Telnyx carries the calls — carrier, phone numbers, transcription, and hosted AI in one vendor and one bill. Supabase (managed Postgres) is the operational brain: the dial queue, the routing rules, and every event of every call as it happens. Snowflake is the long-term memory for analysis. The agent itself — the thing you talked to — is our own code, and it's small on purpose. Nothing in the stack is exotic or licensed per-seat.
How are you running this right now — what launched that call?
One command, which does exactly one thing: it tells the platform "dial this number." After that the laptop is a spectator — answer detection, the greeting, the listening, the decisions, the hang-up all run in the cloud, next to the carrier. Close the lid mid-call and the agent wouldn't notice. In production nothing launches calls by hand: the queue decides who to call and when, under cadence rules, calling-hours compliance, and pacing caps — the demo command and the queue drop the same call into the same machinery.
How do you hook up to LeadConduit? What if we handed you 10,000 leads right now?
Two doors, both framed. Fresh leads: LeadConduit — we become one more recipient endpoint, exactly like the floors are today; point a percentage of any vertical at us and leads arrive in real time, nothing upstream changes. Revive: batch — ten thousand leads is a query and a batch load into the dial queue, which paces them out by calling hours, time zones, and volume caps. Our side of the APIs is built and live; what remains is endpoint verification with our systems team. The software part of "10,000 leads right now" is measured in hours — the gates are operational by design: a seasoned caller-ID pool and each client's transfer config.
What did we build vs. buy?
We buy dial-tone, transcription, and raw AI inference. We built the conversation engine, the queue, the routing, and the measurement — the parts that are the business. A vendor selling us the conversation layer would keep the per-turn data; that data is the product.
What does a call cost?
The expensive part of AI calling is per-minute generated speech, and the soundboard design engineers it out: clips are generated once and replay for free, so we pay AI prices only for decisions and the occasional composed line. Real cost baselines get published with the cost model as live volume ramps.
How do you know it works before real customers hear it?
A synthetic test floor: the agent dials herself in character — buyers, skeptics, ramblers, price shoppers — hundreds of calls per battery. On the current windows script, simulated buyers convert 28 of 28, and the early-hangup monitor has never ended a buyer's call.
What about compliance — TCPA, DNC?
Structural, not scripted. Recorded-line disclosure and consent are must-hit gates the script engine enforces and testing cannot remove. DNC suppression takes effect immediately at dial time, the platform enforces calling hours natively, and every call is recorded and retained five years.
What about spam-labeled caller IDs?
A full number lifecycle is built in: buy scattered singles, screen reputation before the first dial, register during warm-up, monitor health from our own call data with automatic quarantine, retire on schedule and replace. Buying numbers takes minutes — seasoning them is the discipline, and it's a system, not a chore.
Who operates it day-to-day?
The web console: program and pacing controls, script and clip management, lead intake, DNC, and per-call drill-down — every change audited. Daily operation is an ops task, not an engineering task.
When can it take real traffic?
The remaining work is integration verification, not invention — confirming endpoints and authentication with our systems team, then a controlled slice of a real campaign measured on the floors' own KPIs: same disposition vocabulary, same dashboards, apples to apples.
